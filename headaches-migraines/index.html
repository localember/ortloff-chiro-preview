The short answer

Many recurring headaches are driven by the joints and muscles of the upper neck and shoulders rather than by anything in the head itself. Chiropractic care works on that mechanical side: restoring movement where the neck has gotten stiff, easing the muscle tension that builds around it, and giving you the posture and movement habits that keep the pattern from rebuilding.

Headaches get normalised faster than almost anything else we see. People stop counting them, keep a bottle in the desk drawer, and only mention it in passing. Here is a plain-language look at what tends to drive the recurring ones, how chiropractic care can help, and what a course of care actually looks like.

What causes headaches?

Headaches come in types, and they do not all have the same cause. The ones we are best placed to help with are the ones with a mechanical driver: tension-type headaches, and the cervicogenic kind that start in the upper neck and refer pain up into the head. When the joints at the base of the skull stiffen and the muscles around them stay switched on, the result is felt as a headache even though the source sits lower.

The usual contributors we see in Crosslake:

  • Long hours at a screen, with the head drifting forward of the shoulders.
  • A jaw that clenches through stress, or through the night.
  • Sleep that is short, broken, or done on the wrong pillow.
  • Dehydration, skipped meals, and the caffeine that gets used to paper over both.
  • An old neck injury that changed how the upper neck moves.

Some headaches are not mechanical and need a medical work-up rather than hands-on care. A headache that is sudden and severe, that follows a head injury, or that arrives with fever, vision changes, weakness, or confusion is a reason to be seen medically first, not a reason to book with us. Part of a good first visit is telling you which kind you have.

How does chiropractic care help with headaches?

Chiropractic care works on the biomechanics. Gentle, specific adjustments restore movement to the upper neck joints that have gotten stiff and guarded, which eases the pull on the muscles and the tissues that refer pain into the head. Alongside the adjustments, you get simple changes to the things that keep setting the pattern off.

In practice, a course of care usually blends a few things:

  • Specific adjustments to the subluxated vertebrae our analysis finds, in the neck or lower in the spine, matched in force to your body.
  • Targeted movement you can do at home, so the neck holds its range between visits.
  • A look at the practical drivers: screen height, pillow, water, and the hours you keep.

The first goal is to change the pattern, then give you the movement and habits to keep it changed.

How a subluxation can affect the nerves involved

The nerves of the upper neck carry messages to and from the back of the head, the scalp, and the muscles that hold your head up. When a vertebra in the neck is subluxated, it can narrow the opening those nerves pass through and put pressure on them, which can change the messages they carry.

The problem does not always start in the neck. The Gonstead System treats the whole spine as the foundation: when a vertebra or the pelvis lower down is out of position, the spine above it has to compensate, and that strain can carry all the way up to the neck and head. That is why Dr. Luke examines the whole spine for headaches, not only the neck.

Headache questions we hear most

Is chiropractic care safe for headaches?

For most people with common tension-type or neck-driven headaches, gentle chiropractic care has a strong safety record. The first visit includes a careful history and check so care is matched to you, and low-force options are used for necks that are sensitive. If your headache pattern suggests you should be seen medically first, we will tell you rather than start care.

Can chiropractic care help migraines, not just tension headaches?

Sometimes. Many migraine patterns have a neck component, and the research on chiropractic adjustments for migraine is encouraging without being a promise. What we will do is check whether your pattern has a mechanical driver we can work on, and say so honestly if it does not.

How long until my headaches change?

Many people notice a shift in the first few weeks, in frequency before intensity. It depends on how long the pattern has been building and what is feeding it. After your first visit we will give you a realistic window rather than a guess, and we will adjust the plan as it responds.

Do I have to stop my medication?

That is between you and the doctor who prescribed it, and it is not a decision we make. Some people find they reach for it less as the pattern settles, and that is their doctor's call to act on, not ours. We are one part of the picture, not a replacement for the rest of it.

What should I do for a headache right now, before my visit?

Water, a break from the screen, and gentle movement through the neck and shoulders help many people. Note what was happening in the hours before it started, because the pattern is usually more useful than any single episode. If this headache is the worst of your life, came on like a thunderclap, or arrives with fever, vision changes, weakness, or confusion, that is an emergency; call your doctor or go in now.
