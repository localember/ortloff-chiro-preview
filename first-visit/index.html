Gonstead care

Your first visit in Crosslake

Around 45 minutes to an hour, and you will leave knowing what is going on and what happens next.

What happens at a first Gonstead visit?

Around 45 minutes to an hour: your history, a full five-component analysis, imaging where appropriate, and usually your first adjustment.

The first visit is an assessment, not a quick adjustment. The Gonstead method is built on finding the exact segment causing the problem rather than treating the area that hurts, and that takes a proper look.

  1. Your history, before you arrive

    You complete your intake forms online, so the time in the office goes to your spine and not to paperwork.

  2. We talk through your concerns

    What hurts, how long it has been going on, what makes it better or worse, and what you actually want back.

  3. Instrumentation

    A handheld instrument reads skin temperature either side of the spine. Differences point to the levels worth examining closely.

  4. Palpation, static and motion

    We feel for swelling, tenderness and tissue texture, then for how each segment moves. This separates a stiff joint from an irritated one.

  5. X-rays, where appropriate

    Full-spine imaging shows the disc and the alignment directly, so the adjustment is aimed at something measured rather than assumed.

  6. Your first adjustment

    Specific, by hand, to the segment the analysis identified. No adjusting of areas that do not need it.

Why the first visit takes longer

A Gonstead analysis is deliberately slow at the start so that everything after it can be precise. Five separate lines of evidence have to agree before an adjustment is made, and gathering them properly is the difference between correcting a problem and chasing a symptom.

  • Wear something you can move comfortably in.
  • Bring any recent imaging or reports you already have.
  • Allow up to an hour so nothing is rushed.

Common questions

Will I be adjusted on the first visit?

Usually yes, once the analysis is complete. If the findings suggest we should wait for imaging first, we will say so rather than proceed.

Does the adjustment hurt?

A Gonstead adjustment is specific and quick. Most people describe relief rather than pain, and we tell you what we are about to do before we do it.
