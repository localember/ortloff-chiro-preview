Thyroid

Thyroid symptom care in Crosslake

If your thyroid numbers came back acceptable and your symptoms did not go anywhere, that gap is worth looking into properly.

The short answer

Thyroid disease is diagnosed and medicated by your physician, and nothing here changes that. What we offer is a wider assessment for people whose symptoms have outlasted a normal TSH, looking at the surrounding picture rather than one number, and sending you back to your doctor with something useful.

The research on thyroid function keeps moving, and an approach that keeps up with it will see things a single number does not.

Why symptoms can outlast a normal number

TSH is one measure, and it is the one most often used on its own. When it comes back in range the file tends to close, which is a problem if you are still tired, still gaining weight, still foggy. The number being acceptable and you feeling well are not the same finding.

The most common driver of an underactive thyroid is an autoimmune condition, Hashimoto's, and the picture around an autoimmune process is wider than the thyroid itself.

  • Fatigue that sleep does not fix.
  • Weight that will not shift on the same effort that used to work.
  • Brain fog, low mood, and poor tolerance of cold.
  • Hair, skin and digestive changes that arrived alongside the rest.

The idea that addressing the gut alone resolves an autoimmune thyroid picture was widely held and is now understood to be incomplete: helpful for some people, and not the whole story for many others.

What a fuller picture looks at

Rather than a single value, a fuller work-up may span thyroid function, autoimmunity, and metabolic and nutrient status. Which of these is worth running depends on you, and your physician's own results are part of the picture.

In practice that usually means looking across four groups rather than one number.

  • Thyroid function beyond TSH: free T3, free T4, reverse T3, T3 uptake.
  • Thyroid antibodies (TPO and TgAb), which is where an autoimmune pattern shows up.
  • General metabolic and blood panels: CMP, CBC, lipids, LDH.
  • Nutrient and inflammatory status: iron and ferritin, vitamin D, B12 with methylmalonic acid, folate, magnesium, homocysteine, high-sensitivity CRP.

None of that is ordered for its own sake, and a longer list is not automatically a better one. The point is to stop guessing about the part of the picture nobody has looked at yet.

Where this fits with your doctor

Squarely alongside. We do not prescribe thyroid medication, we do not tell anyone to change or stop what they have been prescribed, and if your thyroid needs medical management then it needs your physician. What we can do is assess the wider picture and be clear with you about what we find.

What a first visit involves

  • A full history, with time to actually give it.
  • A look at what has already been run, before anything else is suggested.
  • An honest read on whether there is anything useful here for you.

We cannot promise a specific outcome, and nothing here is a substitute for your physician's care.

About 5 in 100 Americans have hypothyroidism, and Hashimoto's disease is its most common cause, which is why an autoimmune picture is worth looking at rather than assuming.

Source: U.S. National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases (NIDDK), Hashimoto's Disease. niddk.nih.gov

Thyroid questions we hear most

Will you take me off my thyroid medication?

No. Prescribing and adjusting thyroid medication is your physician's, and only your physician's. Nothing we do here changes what you have been prescribed.

My TSH is normal. Is there any point coming in?

That is the most common reason people come in. A normal TSH alongside symptoms that have not shifted is exactly the gap worth looking at.

Do you run the labs, or does my doctor?

Either can, and a lot of what matters may already have been run. We look at what exists before suggesting anything further.

Is this a diagnosis of a thyroid condition?

No. Diagnosing thyroid disease is a medical matter. This is an assessment of the wider picture, and it is meant to go back to your doctor with you.
